# Player Journeys: Insights

Three findings from the **demo dataset** (300 matches on 3 maps, March 3–4, generated by
`datagen/`). The data is synthetic, so these show how the tool supports this kind of analysis;
the numbers describe the demo matches, not a real game. All figures can be reproduced in the
tool (Heatmaps → tick all matches → hover) or from `web/public/data`.

---

## Insight 1: Player combat is overwhelmingly against bots, even with other humans present

### Evidence

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| Matches with 2 or more human players | 248 of 300 (83%) |
| Player kills on bots (`BotKill`) | 2,827 |
| Player kills on other players (`Kill`) | 152 (5.1% of all player kills) |
| How players' matches ended (886 human journeys) | 464 extracted (52%) · 177 killed by bots · 155 killed by players · 90 storm |

### Key finding

Most matches have several humans, yet only about 1 in 20 player kills is player-vs-player.
Bots decide most fights, and they kill more players than other players do.

### Why a level designer should care

Map layout controls how often humans meet. If player-vs-player tension is the intended core of the
game, routes that keep players apart (plus plentiful bots) make it rare.

### Potential design action

Add a few shared objectives or high-value loot spots that pull players onto the same routes; review
bot placement so bots don't absorb most encounters.

### Metrics affected

PvP kill share, time to first player encounter, deaths by cause.

## Insight 2: Saltmarsh Hollow's traffic runs through one crossroads, while the coast is nearly empty

### Evidence

Traffic (different players per area, all 150 Saltmarsh Hollow matches; 452 player journeys):

| Place | Size | Player visits | Share of journeys |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Pump Station (central crossroads) | medium | 282 | 62% |
| Old Cannery | medium | 226 | 50% |
| Salt Flats Depot | large | 189 | 42% |
| Harbor Works | large | 132 | 29% |
| Radio Mast | small | 57 | 13% |
| Lighthouse Point (far coast) | small | 32 | 7% |

### Key finding

The busiest place is not the biggest one but the **junction** where the river roads meet: Pump
Station is visited in 62% of journeys, twice as often as the large Harbor Works. Lighthouse Point
and Radio Mast on the edges are close to dead zones.

### Why a level designer should care

A single crossroads that most players pass through makes matches predictable and concentrates
fights; edge areas that almost nobody visits are wasted space.

### Potential design action

Add a second river crossing to split the flow; give Lighthouse Point a reason to go there (a strong
loot room or an extraction point) or shrink the playable edge.

### Metrics affected

Traffic spread across places, share of the map visited per match, encounter locations.

## Insight 3: The best loot is at the edges, where the storm catches players

### Evidence

| Place | Visits | Loot pickups | Loot per visit | Storm deaths |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Harbor Works (south-west edge) | 132 | 1,813 | 13.7 | 12 |
| Salt Flats Depot (east) | 189 | 1,753 | 9.3 | 7 |
| Pump Station (centre) | 282 | 966 | 3.4 | 1 |

Harbor Works has the most loot per visit on the map and the most storm deaths: 12 of the map's 52
(23%).

### Key finding

The highest reward sits far from the centre, and players who loot there long are the ones the storm
catches. Risk comes from the storm, not from other players.

### Why a level designer should care

Risk and reward are linked, but through travel distance rather than combat. That can feel unfair
(dying to the storm after a long loot) rather than tense.

### Potential design action

Signal the storm's direction earlier, add an extraction point near Harbor Works, or move some
high-value loot towards contested central places.

### Metrics affected

Storm deaths per match, loot per visit by place, extraction rate.

---

### How the numbers were calculated

Places are taken from the map layouts in `datagen/maps.py`; an event counts for a place when it is
within the place's radius. Visits count each player once per place per match. Bot-vs-human
classification and duplicate removal follow the pipeline rules (see ARCHITECTURE.md §4).
